## Design Specificity Verdict

**The Dilemma of Dual Realities**:  
The Braun RT 20 web receiver is suspended between two fundamentally opposing design philosophies: an extraordinary, museum-grade 3D physical radio simulation on one side, and a generic, category-interchangeable 2D web dashboard floating on top of it on the other.

- **LLM Assessment**:  
  The 3D procedural modeling of the radio cabinet, aluminum knurled knobs, perforated speaker grille, needle mechanics, backplate DIN specification label, and superheterodyne audio acoustics (heterodyne carrier whistles, resonant pink noise tracking dial movement, band-shaping filters) is deeply grounded in the 1961 Braun RT 20 and T 1000 aesthetic by Dieter Rams and the Ulm School. It exhibits remarkable historical reverence and technical craft.  
  However, pinned to the top-left is an omnipresent glassmorphic card with duplicate band buttons, duplicate power controls, duplicate digital meters, and a giant orange button. This duplicates what the 3D radio already does physically. Instead of letting the physical object *be* the interface (Rams' 4th principle: *"Good design makes a product understandable... it can make the product talk"*), the UI treats the 3D radio as background eye-candy and forces the user back into generic 2D web widgets. Furthermore, proclaiming `"DESIGN DIETER RAMS / GERMAN PRECISION ACOUSTICS"` in the header banner is self-congratulatory meta-commentary that Dieter Rams himself would never have placed on an instrument.

- **Deterministic Scan**:  
  The automated Impeccable detector flagged **21 warnings** in `index.html` across 6 rules:
  - **`low-contrast` (8 findings)**: Text contrast failures ranging from `2.6:1` to `3.9:1` (WCAG AA requires 4.5:1). Notably, white text on Braun safety orange (`#ffffff` on `#ff5500`) achieves only **3.2:1** on the primary CTA button (`TURN ON`, `index.html:72`). Inactive badges (`STANDBY` at **3.1:1** and `OFFLINE` at **2.6:1**) fail legibility thresholds.
  - **`undersized-ui-text` (6 findings)**: Micro-typography at `9.5px` and `10px` on badges, labels, and meter readings (`index.html:35, 36, 52, 53, 63, 64`) violating the 11px functional floor.
  - **`layout-transition` (2 findings in HTML + 1 in CSS)**: Realtime audio and signal meters animate `transition: width 0.1s ease` (`style.css:236`), inducing main-thread layout reflow at up to 60fps against Three.js WebGL frame timing.

  - **`cream-palette` (1 finding)**: Page background `#dedad4` flagged as generic beige slop. While historically motivated by Braun's light grey / off-white palette, it causes figure-ground separation failure with the classic white radio variant and fails to adjust in Night Mode.

## Overall Impression

An extraordinary, acoustically authentic 3D simulation of Dieter Rams' 1961 Braun RT 20 trapped beneath an overly talkative, redundant web HUD. The single biggest opportunity is to **distill the interface**: eliminate the dueling controls, let the tactile 3D radio be the sole primary instrument, and convert the web UI into a quiet, whisper-level secondary companion.

## What's Working

1. **Acoustic & Superheterodyne Simulation**:  
   The audio architecture (`src/audioEngine.js`) is masterclass work. Generating pink noise that shifts resonant cutoff with dial position, modeling the heterodyne carrier whistle that sweeps in pitch toward zero-beat, and applying band-specific filtering (vintage 4.5kHz AM bandwidth vs 18kHz FM) creates genuine analog verisimilitude.
2. **Procedural PBR Texturing & Mechanical Authenticity**:  
   The canvas texture generators in `src/textures.js` capture Ulm School typographic precision: micro-perforated speaker grills with chamfered specular highlights, knurled cylinder normal maps, and a detailed rear spec plate adhering to DIN 41524 standards.
3. **Reactive Physical Animation in 3D Space**:  
   Binding the Web Audio FFT analyser to the physical speaker cone excursion, the dial lamp glow, and the mechanical VU galvanometer needle creates a living acoustic sculpture.

## Priority Issues

### [P0] Dueling Interfaces: The 2D HUD Cannibalizes the 3D Radio
- **Why it matters**: Violates Dieter Rams' core doctrine (*"Weniger, aber besser"*). By duplicating power, band selection, tuning readouts, and VU meters into a floating 2D card, the interface demotes the 3D radio to inert background decoration and clutters the viewport with competing cognitive demands.
- **Fix**: Distill the UI. Demote or auto-hide the floating HUD card into a minimal now-playing ticker. Make the 3D radio the sole primary instrument for power, bands, and volume.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable distill`

### [P1] Broken Rotary Knob Kinematics
- **Why it matters**: Knobs are the primary tactile interface of an analog receiver. Calculating rotation from linear screen drag (`deltaX + deltaY`) feels unnatural and unpredictable, frustrating fine-tuning and driving users away from physical interaction.
- **Fix**: Calculate rotational angle dynamically using `Math.atan2()` relative to knob center projected to screen coordinates, with calibrated tactile notch detents and smooth wheel scrolling.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable polish`

### [P1] Complete Keyboard & Accessibility Exclusion
- **Why it matters**: Power users cannot navigate without a mouse, and assistive technology users (screen readers, keyboard-only navigation) are completely locked out of the experience. Furthermore, text contrast (`3.2:1` on primary buttons) and `9.5px`–`10px` micro-text fail WCAG AA.
- **Fix**: Add universal keyboard shortcuts (`Space` = power toggle, `ArrowLeft/Right` = tune/seek, `1–4` = band select, `M` = mute, `V` = cycle camera), make 3D parts focusable, inject ARIA live announcements for frequency and station lock, and bump font sizes/contrast to pass the craft floor.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable adapt`

### [P2] Silent Audio Stream Failures
- **Why it matters**: Public Icecast streams inevitably suffer network drops or CORS blocks. Silent failure leaves users thinking the app is broken rather than handling radio atmospheric conditions.
- **Fix**: Introduce graceful error states: if buffering exceeds 4.5s or stream errors occur, display a subtle amber status indicator on the dial and automatically fall back to an offline procedural audio loop or alternate stream.
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable harden`

### [P2] Performance Thrashing via `transition: width` on Realtime Meters
- **Why it matters**: High-frequency updates on `.meter-fill` trigger repeated main-thread layout recalculations on every audio frame, competing directly with Three.js WebGL rendering.
- **Fix**: Replace `transition: width` with compositor-friendly GPU transforms (`transform: scaleX(...)` with `transform-origin: left`).
- **Suggested command**: `/impeccable optimize`

## Persona Red Flags

- **Alex (Impatient Power User)**:  
  *Core failure*: Complete lack of keyboard controls. Alex expects to hit `Space` to start music and `Arrow` keys to seek stations. Instead, Alex is forced to manually drag knobs with a mouse or click through nested menus. High risk of immediate tab abandonment.
- **Jordan (Confused First-Timer)**:  
  *Core failure*: Ambiguous locus of control. Jordan sees two "Turn On" buttons (one 3D rocker switch, one bright orange HUD button) and doesn't know which is canonical. When tuning produces harsh heterodyne squeals, Jordan thinks the web app is glitching rather than simulating radio physics. Furthermore, opening the station drawer leaves Jordan trapped because pressing `Esc` or clicking outside fails to close it.
- **Sam (Accessibility-Dependent User)**:  
  *Core failure*: Total `<canvas>` black box. Screen readers encounter an empty canvas with no accessible DOM nodes, no ARIA roles or live regions, and no indication that a station has locked. Low contrast text (`#747880` on `#dedad4`, `3.2:1`) and `9.5px` text fail basic accessibility thresholds.

## Minor Observations

- **Incandescent Thermal Rise**: Dial lamp instantly jumps between 0 and 1.8 intensity; a 150ms cubic-ease warm-up and 300ms cool-down would authentically emulate a vacuum-tube / tungsten filament bulb.
- **Flat Wood Material**: The "Wood" theme uses a flat hexadecimal color `#d4be96` without grain, looking like cardboard rather than fine mid-century Scandinavian ash or walnut veneer.
- **Missing Volume/Tone Detents**: While tuning has audible tick feedback, volume and tone rotate silently, making them feel less tactile.
- **Self-Congratulatory Meta-UI**: The top banner proclaiming `"DESIGN DIETER RAMS / GERMAN PRECISION ACOUSTICS"` contradicts Rams' ethos of unpretentious, quiet tools.
